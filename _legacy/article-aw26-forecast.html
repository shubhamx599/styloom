AW26 Forecast: The Ten Signals
We're Watching Now

Before the AW26 shows begin, the signals are already in the culture. Here is what CONTOUR is tracking — and what we expect each signal to become by the time autumn hits the runway.

Priya Nair

Head of Trend Intelligence · Contour

AW26 Preview Editorial — Contour · 2026

Every season, CONTOUR tracks the early indicators that will shape what appears on the runway three to six months later. These signals come from multiple sources — street observations in key cities, material choices from fabric trade shows, early-stage designer conversations, cultural shifts in how people are talking about dress. When several signals point in the same direction, we publish what we're seeing.

Here are the ten AW26 signals we are currently tracking most closely.

01. Weight Returns

After several seasons of lightness — sheer fabrics, tissue-weight wools, deliberate fragility — we're seeing a clear shift toward garments with physical presence. Heavy wool, dense cashmere, structured canvas. The body wants to feel clothed again, not draped. Weight as intentional statement.

02. Colour Restraint Breaks in One Direction

The neutral palettes of the last two seasons are not disappearing — but one colour is being admitted selectively into otherwise austere wardrobes. Deep, specific reds. Not trend-reds. Considered, architectural reds that function as the single departure point in an otherwise controlled palette.

"AW26 will not be a dramatic pivot. It will be a deepening — the same direction as SS26, but with more formal commitment and darker material vocabulary."

03. The Coat as the Collection

In several of the strongest AW26 preview pieces we've seen, the coat is no longer outerwear — it's the garment. The thing around which the entire outfit is organised. Designers are putting the most technical investment, the most considered proportions, and the most specific fabric choices into the outer layer. Everything underneath is secondary.

04. Texture Over Pattern

Surface interest is coming from material rather than print. Bouclé, brushed wool, technical tweed, ribbed knit — all of them creating visual complexity through texture rather than graphic decoration. This is consistent with the broader technical investment we're seeing across the strongest SS26 pieces.

05. Precision Tailoring Without Formality

The suit is not coming back in its traditional form. What's emerging is more interesting: the precision of tailoring applied to garments that don't signal "office" or "occasion." Tailored proportions in casual contexts. The cut and construction of formal dress applied to everyday wear. Formal thinking, informal wearing.

Precision Tailoring Study — Contour · AW26 Preview

06. Footwear Grounds the Silhouette

The SS26 silhouette — elongated, wide at the shoulder, vertical in its emphasis — needs specific footwear to work. We're seeing a move toward footwear with physical weight and a connection to the ground. Flat, structured, purposeful. The elevated heel is retreating. The shoe is becoming architectural rather than decorative.

07. Interior Detail Returns

One of the most interesting signals we're tracking is a renewed interest in the inside of a garment. Lined coats where the lining is as considered as the exterior. Interior construction visible at collar and cuff. The quality that previously only the wearer could see is becoming part of the statement. Private craft made quietly public.

08. Knitwear as Architecture

Knitwear this autumn is structural rather than soft. The signals from material trade shows are pointing toward dense, precise knits — technically demanding to produce, impossible to fast-fashion convincingly. This is consistent with the overall direction: quality that cannot be easily replicated.

09. The Accessory Disappears

The most significant accessory signal for AW26 is negative: the deliberate reduction of accessories. The strongest AW26 preview looks we've seen are complete without addition. No statement bag, no layered jewellery. The garment is sufficient. This is perhaps the clearest single indicator that the direction of travel is toward presence over decoration.

10. The Waist Finds Itself

SS26 suggested the waist rather than defined it. AW26, we believe, will take a more explicit position. Not the corseted extremes of recent years — something more architectural. A waist that exists as a structural point in the silhouette, defined by cut and proportion rather than restriction. The body acknowledged, not imposed upon.
